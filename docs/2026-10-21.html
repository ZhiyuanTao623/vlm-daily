A Multi-Agent Framework for Automated Machine Learning with Evidence-based Planning for Medical Image Analysis

2026-10-20 cs.CV Johns Hopkins arXiv:2610.28410v1

Yiqing Shen, Mathias Unberath

Deep learning has become the default tool for medical image analysis, driven by the open-source movement. However, achieving the best performance on a given task remains difficult, with architecture selection and hyperparameter optimization (HPO) still considered "more of an art than a science". Automated machine learning (AutoML) automates parts of model training, yet helps little in medical imaging: general-purpose approaches lack medical architectures and preprocessing, and medical-focused ones demand engineering expertise and remain confined to narrow tasks such as 3D segmentation. We introduce MedicalMLOmni, a multi-agent AutoML framework that automates model training for diverse single- and multi-modal medical tasks through a natural language interface. Specifically, MedicalMLOmni performs evidence-based planning by retrieving published results to guide model selection and configuration, and employs parallel HPO where multiple agents explore adjustment strategies concurrently. MedicalMLOmni achieves the best score on 19 of 20 benchmarks; PMC-VQA is the exception, where a vision-language model with broader pre-training leads. Backbone-matched controls attribute the gains to the framework, not the backbone. By removing coding requirements, MedicalMLOmni lowers the barrier for domain experts with rich datasets but limited engineering expertise.

DIVE: Benchmarking Full-Duplex Interaction with Visual Engagement in Continuous Streams

2026-10-20 cs.AI Princeton arXiv:2610.28405v1

Yuhang Chen, Guangyue Liu, Shilong Liu

Recent advances in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have substantially improved spoken interaction and video understanding. Existing online and streaming benchmarks primarily assess causal multimodal perception through timestamped question answering, whereas real-world interaction requires models to continuously identify task-relevant multimodal cues and decide whether, when, and how to respond. This leaves open how well current models can sustain real-time interaction throughout an evolving multimodal stream. To address this gap, we introduce DIVE, a benchmark for evaluating full-duplex interaction with visual engagement under continuous and realistic multimodal streams. It organizes six tasks into three complementary interaction scenarios. (1) Proactive Intervention evaluates whether models can identify emerging visual or semantic cues and intervene at the appropriate moment. (2) Continuous Engagement evaluates sustained participation through continuous visual tracking and visually grounded query interaction. (3) Interaction Coordination evaluates how models coordinate listening and speaking under overlapping and multi-party interactions. DIVE contains 525 audio/video samples with fine-grained human annotations and precise temporal metadata. Its evaluation protocol separates response quality, participation decisions, and response timing. We evaluate representative real-time models using response quality, window response rates, and reaction latency. Experiments show that leading real-time models achieve sub-second average response latency, yet their average window response rates remain at or below 53.77%. Response quality varies substantially across models and tasks, with adapting ongoing responses to newly arriving information remaining particularly challenging. We hope DIVE facilitates progress toward more natural and capable real-time multimodal interactive systems.

Gated Prototype Transfer for Zero-Support Medical Vision Language Model

2026-10-20 cs.CV Harvard arXiv:2610.28350v1

Sumaiya Fazal, Rizwan Qureshi, Laleh Behjat, Samira Ebrahimi Kahou

Realistic few-shot adaptation of medical vision-language models (VLMs) can leave low-prevalence classes without any labeled support. The training-free SS- Text+ solver remains operational in this setting, but its zero-support prototypes receive no visual adaptation and fall back to a purely textual class prototype. We in- troduce Gated Knowledge-Guided Prototype Transfer (GProtoT), a pre-repulsion module that transfers supported-class visual residuals to missing classes using VLM similarity and an ontology-based clinical distance. A validation-free evidence gate scales transfer based on semantic relevance and residual agreement, thereby limiting unsupported extrapolation. In 50 paired realistic one-shot trials on MESSIDOR with FLAIR features, severe and proliferative diabetic retinopathy were absent from every support set. Relative to SS-Text+, gated ProtoT improved missing-class recall by 5.23 points, average class accuracy by 1.37 points, and the missing/supported recall harmonic mean by 1.83 points. It reduced negative- transfer frequency from 46-48% for ungated transfer to 2%, while at the cost of reductions of 1.20 percentage points in supported-class recall and 0.45 percentage points in top-1 accuracy. These results indicate that evidence-based gating provides a safety mechanism for the knowledge source. GProtoT is based on SS- Text+ and requires no gradients, validation data, or additional model inference, of- fering a low-compute path toward accountable adaptation under incomplete clini- cal supervision. Code is available: https://github.com/sumaiyaafridi/GProtoT

Auto-Director: Leveraging Multi-Agent Collaboration for Autonomous Video Editing

2026-10-20 cs.CV UC Berkeley arXiv:2610.28346v1

Yuxia Chen, Wenbo Zhu, Yangguang Ji, Zhenxiang Jiang, Teng Zhong, Yu Zhang

Autonomous video editing from open-world resources remains a challenging problem in multimedia content creation. Retrieval-based methods are constrained by fixed source material and lack creative flexibility, while generative models struggle with long-horizon coherence and often diverge from user intent. Both paradigms fail to replicate the structured, multi-stage reasoning that underlies professional post-production. To address these limitations, we present Auto-Director, a tool-augmented multi-agent framework that mirrors professional editorial workflows: a Planning Agent decomposes abstract topics into fact-grounded scripts via retrieval-augmented planning; a Material Agent indexes open-world footage through shot segmentation and visual tagging; an Editor Agent assembles semantically aligned clips with MLLM-driven spatial adaptation; and a Critic Agent governs an iterative Reflect-and-Revise loop that checks scene-level alignment and pacing. To rigorously evaluate this new paradigm, we introduce VEdit-Bench, a benchmark of 100 expert-curated topics with human-annotated Golden Scripts and a five-dimensional metric suite covering Narrative Logic, Factual Accuracy, Semantic Alignment, Compositional Quality, and Stylistic Cohesion. Extensive experiments demonstrate that Auto-Director significantly outperforms baseline methods in both automated metrics and human evaluation, effectively bridging the gap between amateurs and professional-grade video production.

Steering Vision-Language Models for Visual-Contextual Privacy in Location Disclosure

2026-10-19 cs.CV University of WashingtonGeorgia TechDuke arXiv:2610.27323v1

Wenyue Chen, Yuhan Cheng, Ruixin Yang, Ethan Mendes, Alan Ritter, Jingwei Sun, Hai Li, Yiran Chen

Vision-language models (VLMs) can infer sensitive information such as geolocation from subtle visual cues, creating privacy risks when responses disclose more detail than the visual context warrants. We study visual-contextual privacy in geolocation disclosure, where a VLM should reveal location information only at the granularity appropriate to the visual context depicted in the image. We first show that this contextually appropriate granularity is recoverable from pre-generation hidden states, even when generated responses are misaligned, with disclosure levels exhibiting an ordered structure along a common hidden-state direction. Motivated by this, we propose an inference-time route-and-steer framework: an ordinal granularity router maps visual-contextual privacy representations to a disclosure route, and a disclosure-level-conditioned steering module applies route-specific hidden-state interventions during decoding. Across four VLM backbones, our method improves visual-context-aware disclosure alignment, better balances over- and under-disclosure, and incurs only modest changes on general capability benchmarks. It further transfers to another dataset with a distinct data distribution and privacy-risk taxonomy.

Robustness of Internal Signals for Hallucination Detection in Vision-Language Models

2026-10-19 cs.CV Princeton arXiv:2610.27041v1

Allison Chen, William Yang, Salma Abdel Magid, Jonathan Williams, Olga Russakovsky, Esin Tureci

When given an image and a text prompt, vision-language models (VLMs) can produce seemingly sophisticated text responses. However, they are also prone to hallucinate objects in their output, which can have detrimental consequences in safety-critical applications. To detect these hallucinations, certain methods interestingly leverage internal signals from the VLM as it generates the text response; potentially, these methods use the same signals that led to a hallucination to detect it. We conduct an in-depth analysis to understand the robustness of these detection methods. First, we show that a naive baseline of a generated word's token position in the VLM text response achieves competitive performance. Further, we reveal how some detection methods rely on token position and use this heuristic as a shortcut. Second, we explore detection methods' sensitivity to language and vision modalities. Our experiments suggest that strong detection performance is often driven by detecting language-based hallucinations, and when methods rely on token position, they tend to be less visually grounded and exhibit reduced robustness when hallucinations occur earlier in a text response. Lastly, we demonstrate that different detection methods can carry complementary signals; when ensembling methods together, adding weaker methods to an ensemble of higher performing ones still offers performance improvements. Altogether, while there may be useful internal VLM signals to detect hallucinations, our investigation reveals important limitations of the current methods that try to leverage them.
