AesCode: Aesthetic Code Generation with Decoupled Cross-Modal Rewards

2026-10-13 cs.AI Yale arXiv:2610.18860v1

Xin Gao, Yifan Yang, Xin Li, Pan Wang, Yan Li, Muzhao Tian, Mingxi Cheng, Bei Liu et al.

Generating information-rich visual artifacts such as slides, posters, and dashboards as code is appealing because the outputs are structured, editable, and verifiable. Yet code models cannot see how their choices of layout and color come together on the canvas, often limiting spatial planning and aesthetic quality. Image generators excel at visual design but can misrender requested content, making their outputs unreliable targets for direct reproduction. We therefore introduce AesCode, which generates code grounded in the content prompt while using an image generated from the same prompt as an aesthetic guidance. To do so, AesCode represents the target as a design graph that describes the whole canvas and makes each property attributable. From this graph, we derive complementary cross-modal rewards: deterministic verifiers score properties parsable from code and rendering, while a VLM judge assesses the non-parsable ones with a sample-specific Visual Graph Rubric (VGR) tied to graph elements and relations. We optimize these decoupled rewards through reinforcement learning with per-channel normalization to jointly improve spatial planning, aesthetic quality, and verifiable correctness. To evaluate this setting, we build a benchmark that scores verifiable correctness and visual quality separately, with visual scores highly consistent with human judgments. We find that reference images substantially improve the visual quality of code generated by existing models, and ablations show that AesCode benefits from references both as model input and as a source of visual rewards. Relative to same-scale Qwen3-VL baselines given the same references, AesCode-8B and AesCode-32B improve visual quality by 31.1 and 22.4 percentage points, reaching scores comparable to those of closed-source frontier models such as GPT-5.5 and Claude Opus 4.8.

SP$^2$ec: Adaptive Self-Speculative Decoding for Vision-Language Models

2026-10-13 cs.LG Yale arXiv:2610.18735v1

Yuqi Huang, Xingyao Li, Yunlong Hou, Fengzhuo Zhang, Jiachun Pan, Vincent Y. F. Tan

Speculative Decoding (SD) has demonstrated its efficacy in accelerating large language model inference, but for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), the diverse input modalities make it difficult for a small trained drafter to achieve consistently high throughput. Self-speculative decoding reuses parts of the target model as a drafter which retains the multi-modal capability of the VLMs, yet its efficiency depends on choosing a suitable draft configuration for each prompt. We propose Single-Peak SPeculative decoding (SP$^2$ec), a training-free bandit algorithm that adapts the draft configuration during generation. SP$^2$ec exploits the single-peak throughput pattern observed in existing self-speculative methods, including SWIFT, KnapSpec, SpecVLM, and our simple cosSim. Our algorithmic design explicitly minimizes a novel notion of wall-time regret for each given query, corresponding to the empirical wall-time latency. Theoretical guarantees on the wall-time regret show that SP$^2$ec can exploit the optimal draft configuration, with negligible exploration cost. Extensive experiments on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral-7B across visual and textual tasks show consistent improvements of up to 11\% over the original self-speculative methods, demonstrating the wide applicability of the proposed SP$^2$ec algorithm.

CREATE: Creativity Evaluation of VLMs using the Visual Remote Associates Test

2026-10-13 cs.CL Columbia arXiv:2610.18569v1

Ziqing Yang, Anubhav Jangra, Smaranda Muresan

Vision language models (VLMs) are used in creative tasks such as visual storytelling, design ideation, art interpretation, and advertising. Yet we lack principled ways to measure the lateral reasoning of these models compared to human potential. We introduce CREATE, a semi-automatically constructed benchmark containing 155 visual Remote Associates Test problems. These problems require a solver to find an underlying connection among three seemingly dissimilar images. We conduct a human study (N=25) to validate and ground the quality of our benchmark, and evaluate 11 VLMs. Our findings show that most VLMs underperform humans on this task. Our experiments indicate that the associative ability of VLMs depends on the cue concepts provided in the text and does not universally extend to visual puzzles.

EgoOmniWorld: Unifying Egocentric Understanding via Ego-Action-World Interactions

2026-10-13 cs.CV Carnegie Mellon arXiv:2610.18499v1

Songruo Chen, Yuqian Fu, Yu Li, Mingkang Dong, Sifan Zhou, Bin Ren, Xu Zheng, Tianwen Qian et al.

Egocentric video understanding spans diverse capabilities, modalities, and evaluation formats, yet these are often studied in isolation despite their tight coupling in real-world interaction. We propose the Ego-Action-World interaction loop as a unifying principle: the wearer perceives the world, forms goals, acts, changes the world, and uses feedback to guide subsequent behavior. Based on this view, we introduce EgoOmniWorld, a benchmark organized around four complementary interaction structures: Node captures ego, action, and world states; Edge captures directed relations among them; Chain captures linked dependencies; and Loop captures feedback-closed interactions. We develop an interaction-first, evidence-grounded data pipeline that extracts structured interactions from egocentric videos, grounds them in visual and auditory evidence, and generates questions according to their interaction structure. Required modalities and single- or multi-turn formats follow naturally from the supporting evidence and dependencies. EgoOmniWorld contains 24,166 QA groups, including 20,284 training and 3,882 test samples. Experiments on representative open- and closed-source multimodal large language models reveal substantial and uneven limitations across interaction structures. Models trained on EgoOmniWorld improve in-domain interaction understanding and generalize to existing egocentric benchmarks. EgoOmniWorld provides a unified foundation for evaluating and advancing egocentric video understanding. Data, code, and models will be released.

Teaching Vision-Language Models to Decline: Support Completion Against Mirage

2026-10-12 cs.CV Purdue arXiv:2610.16465v1

Alfaj Uddin Ahmed, Md. Shaown Miah, Md Arid Hasan, Syed Ishtiaque Ahmed, Sayeed Shafayet Chowdhury

Vision-language models answer questions about images they were never shown, a failure called mirage. Such an answer can be correct without being justified by the evidence, and the difference matters wherever a model's output is checked by someone who did not see its input. We locate the failure in the training objective: cross-entropy separates a model that reads the image from one that ignores it by only the conditional mutual information between answer and image, and no training example rewards declining. We formalise support completion, TIDE, which also shows each training question with a blank image, a same-domain wrong image and an out-of-domain image, with targets that decline, and prove that under TIDE every image-blind predictor is suboptimal by at least 1.04 nats per example, that the optimum declines exactly on the counterfactual kinds while keeping its behaviour on valid images, and that for any finite model the error of its answer-or-decline decision, declining on valid input plus answering on invalid input, is bounded below by how well its representation separates valid from invalid evidence. On Qwen2.5-VL-7B under a frozen held-out protocol with two judges, the three-condition predecessor of TIDE reduced the invalid-evidence mirage rate from 0.759 to 0.097 and the blank-image rate from 0.700 to 0.003 at a false-abstention cost of 0.126; label-free grounding raised the rate to 0.859, a likelihood-advantage contrast moved it only to 0.718 because its empty-image term carries no gradient, and grounding applied after support completion re-opened the mirage. The four-condition protocol on three base models, with seeds and component ablations, is in progress and not reported here.

Elephant in the Fridge: Constant-Memory Frame Packing for Long Video Understanding

2026-10-12 cs.CV University of WashingtonUNC Chapel Hill arXiv:2610.16118v1

Shuo Gao, Chenhao Zheng, Zhongzheng Ren

Hour-long videos, streaming feeds, and movie-length content are pushing vision-language models (VLMs) past their breaking point -- the standard recipe of encoding each frame into visual tokens yields sequences that grow linearly with video length, causing prohibitive memory consumption and inference latency. Existing approaches, such as token reduction and keyframe selection, shorten the token sequence but leave the O(n) scaling intact, failing to resolve the fundamental bottleneck. We propose FramePack-U, a strikingly simple, training-free frame packing framework that compresses arbitrarily long videos into a fixed size visual representation at constant memory cost. FramePack-U requires no fine-tuning or architectural modification -- it plugs directly into off-the-shelf VLMs at inference time. Inspired by FramePack from the generation literature, we propose a content-aware compression strategy for visual understanding under a fixed token budget. Specifically, FramePack-U (1) scores each frame by its semantic similarity to the text prompt and ranks frames in a diversity-driven manner, and (2) packs the selected frames at varying resolutions into a fixed-length context window. This concentrates the memory budget on frames that are both instruction-relevant and visually diverse. While FramePack-U can in theory pack arbitrarily many frames into the fixed budget, we identify an empirical instantiation that generalizes robustly across settings. Extensive experiments on LongVideoBench, MLVU, and Video-MME show that FramePack-U consistently outperforms full-resolution baselines and prior token reduction and keyframe selection methods across diverse model families, parameter scales, and frame budgets. Notably, on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B with a 128-frame budget, FramePack-U yields gains of +12.9\%, +10.6\%, and +9.7\% over the official model on the three benchmarks, respectively.

Meaningful Moments: A Corpus of VLM-Derived Temporal Importance Labels for Video Action Recognition

2026-10-12 cs.CV Dartmouth arXiv:2610.16009v1

Cameron Keith, SouYoung Jin

Not every moment in a video is equally useful for recognizing an action. A few decisive moments often carry the evidence: the instant an object moves, the peak of a dive, or one object placed on another. Existing video datasets rarely provide large-scale, action-conditioned labels for which short segments carry that evidence. This work asks whether vision-language models can identify meaningful moments at scale and whether those labels improve action recognition.We introduce Meaningful Moments (MM), a corpus of about 4.58 million VLM-generated per-segment importance scores for nearly 500,000 class-labeled videos spanning Something-Something v2 (SSv2), Kinetics-400 (K400), and Diving-48 across 622 action classes. We freeze the action classifier and vary only temporal sampling. An alpha-parameterized comparison of importance-led selection against an importance-inverted control separates gains from the VLM signal from gains caused by variable-density sampling alone. Across all three datasets, VLM-derived importance is informative: at the hard-cut endpoint, keeping VLM-important segments outperforms keeping VLM-designated filler. Usefulness depends on class structure. On Diving-48, importance-led fast-forwarding preserves full-video recognition while the inverted control collapses by roughly 30 percentage points. On K400, hard-cut selection beats uniform sampling by 1.45 percentage points, rising to 2.05 under threshold selection. On SSv2, the labels carry signal but do not recover full-video performance. Direct scoring matches greedy removal within its noise floor at roughly 25 times fewer oracle calls, and a four-VLM cross-oracle study shows consistent downstream trends. We release the importance labels and evaluation protocol under CC-BY-4.0; SSv2 class strings remain under CC-BY-NC-4.0.

Attention Sinks in VLMs Are Borrowed, Not Seen

2026-10-11 cs.AI Johns HopkinsVanderbilt arXiv:2610.15855v1

Dengjia Zhang, Alexander Martin, Pengyu Yan, Xihan Sun, Jiaqi Li, Benjamin Van Durme, Andrew Yates, Reno Kriz et al.

We study how to detect and mitigate visual attention sinks in vision-language models (VLMs). Visual attention sinks are image tokens that absorb a large share of attention over the image regardless of the query or task, and they mislead methods that treat attention maps as a record of where the model ``looked.'' Prior work has detected sinks through massive activation dimensions and proposed redistributing their attention, but none have addressed what makes a token become a sink. Comparing six candidate detectors on ten VLMs from four families, we find that only the centered cosine similarity between an image token's key and the key of the language model's text-sink detects visual sinks. Thus, a visual sink is an image token whose key aligns with the text-sink's key. Moving a sink's key component along this direction to the median of the image keys sends its attention back to the text-sink (\textbf{Are Borrowed}) while leaving model answers unchanged~(\textbf{Not Seen}). Building on this, we propose a training-free, entropy-triggered re-look that first cleans the attention map, then crops the most-attended region of the cleaned map and inserts it into the reasoning trace. Cleaning adds negligible computation to the forward pass, and the re-look improves accuracy with a mean gain of $+4.3$ points on fine-detail VQA benchmarks across model families.

Semantic Saliency from Multi-Modal Large Language Model Scene Understanding Maps

2026-10-11 cs.CV UC Santa Barbara arXiv:2610.15467v1

Ziqi Wen, Shravan Murlidaran, Jonathan Skaza, William Wang, Miguel P. Eckstein

Image-computable low-level image saliency has shaped perceptual psychology and computer science, but is limited by its inability to capture human high-level cognitive factors that influence attention and eye movements. Here, we propose a new fully automated method to compute semantic saliency by quantifying each object's contribution to scene comprehension without fitting any parameters to human data: the Automated Scene Understanding Map (AUTO-SUM). AUTO-SUM automatically segments and removes individual objects from a scene, uses multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to describe the original and modified scenes, and measures the semantic similarity between these descriptions using text embeddings. We show that AUTO-SUM can predict which object is most important in a human-measured scene understanding map (H-SUM) estimated using human scene descriptions. We also show that AUTO-SUM can predict the object most fixated by human observers during free viewing and scene description tasks better than a low-level saliency model (GBVS, Itti-Koch), a saliency model trained based on human meaning ratings (Meaning Map), and comparable to DeepGaze, which is trained on human fixation data. We contend that AUTO-SUM can be used as a semantic saliency model that complements lower-level saliency models. Code are available at https://github.com/starsky77/AUTO-SUM-Source-Code .

EvolvAttack: Backdooring self Evolving VLM Agents

2026-10-11 cs.LG UPennBrown arXiv:2610.15244v1

Chuan Zou, Yang Xiang, Zexin Li, Xiaonan Luo, Simin Chen

Self-evolving mechanisms let vision-language model (VLM) agents continually adapt through evolving memory, but they also allow malicious behavior to emerge only after deployment. We introduce a new attack surface, the self-evolving backdoor, whose payload stays dormant in the initial agent and is progressively activated as the agent evolves. Unlike conventional backdoors, which can be exposed by testing triggered inputs on a static model, our attack requires both a visual trigger and a sufficiently evolved memory state; static scanning with clean memory thus yields near-zero attack success and may deem the agent benign. We realize this through memory-aware backdoor training, which couples trigger activation with the agent's evolving memory while preserving benign behavior unless the trigger co-occurs with payload-related memory. Across three VLM backbones, four agent pipelines, and recency-, semantic-, and reward-based memory evolution, the attack preserves normal functionality and stays nearly inactive before evolution (0.5% attack success rate), yet exceeds 95% attack success within 100 interactions, revealing a stealthy threat unique to self-evolving agents.

Understanding Unified VLMs: From Attention Probing to Guidance Tuning for Multimodal Generation

2026-10-10 cs.AI University of MichiganOhio State arXiv:2610.15227v1

Zhengyang Luo, Xiaoyu Qiu, Zhiheng Tan, Yuchen Liang, Yingbin Liang, Liyue Shen

Unified vision-language models (VLMs) perform both text-to-image (T2I) and image-to-text (I2T) generation with one shared Transformer backbone, yet how the backbone processes the condition in either direction remains poorly understood. We study this question from the perspective of conditional guidance: we probe every attention head to show the activation difference between two branches of classifier-free guidance (CFG) with different conditional guidance. On two unified VLMs of autoregressive and masked-diffusion paradigms, the probing reveals a subset of heads that are more sensitive to the change of condition while others are agnostic to it. This characteristic pattern holds in both T2I and I2T generation and is largely stable across data samples, demonstrating the internal mechanism of unified VLMs. Motivated by this, we propose a new inference-time intervention approach for unified multimodal generation, named \textit{HeadTuned-CFG}, which uses the probed condition-change awareness to select and tune the conditional guidance at the attention-head level. Experiments show consistent benefits in both multimodal generation directions of unified VLMs without additional training, and in particular substantial improvements over strong baselines in T2I generation. These results further support the key finding of our probing analysis: the observed pattern of functional specialization is an intrinsic property of unified VLMs.

Swarm Telepathy: Prefill-Free KV-Cache Transfer Across Families

2026-10-10 cs.LG MIT arXiv:2610.15095v1

Abdulrahman Alabdulkareem, Irene Tenison, Yang Zhang, Yada Zhu, Boris Katz, Dan Gutfreund

LLM multi-agent systems split work across agents that communicate through text. An agent summarizes its findings, another agent receives the text. This constrains communication to discrete tokens and costs redundant compute, limiting bandwidth and capabilities of multi-agent systems. Recent works demonstrate KV-cache transfer between LLMs but they either require the LLMs to be within the same family or cannot function across context, thus requiring the receiver to prefill text. We present SwaT (Swarm Telepathy), the first cross-family KV-cache transfer method that allows prefill-free communication in multi-family multi-agent systems. In an agent-to-agent communication setting, combining SwaT with recent advances in KV-cache compaction beats text-to-text communication in both accuracy and speed, with a mean of $40\%$ higher accuracy and a mean of $4\times$ faster wall-clock time. Experiments show SwaT achieves high performance on 17 benchmarks across 12 LLMs and 6 families. Additionally, given a sender Vision-Language Model (VLM), SwaT is able to transfer the vision capabilities to a text-only receiver LLM exceeding text-to-text communication despite SwaT being trained on text-only data. SwaT keeps both sender and receiver models frozen without fine-tuning and only requires $<50M$ tokens and $<1$ GPU-day to train.
